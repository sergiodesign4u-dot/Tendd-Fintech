Landing auth

The way in, as the public page draws it: the sign-in panel in three steps, the two doors of Get started in the same card, the dialog that holds either over the landing, and the page that holds them everywhere else.

Organism .lp-auth .lp-start .lp-modal .lp-gate 0 grey pages step: choose / email / sent. host: dialog / page added 2026-09-27 (P17)

What it is

The founder found the sign-in screen dull and wordy and asked for the action in front: Google first, with its mark, email as a second button that leads to the field, in a dialog rather than a separate page, and the explanations moved to the privacy policy and the terms (P17). The panel is node 1.6 in three steps, choose, email and sent; the start panel is node 1.2, two doors drawn identically (D2) with a picture on each.

One card, two hosts. On the landing a plain click on any link to sign in or to get started opens a native <dialog> over the page, which holds still under it. The links stay links, so a new tab or a page with no script reaches /sign-in and /path-choice, where the same card stands on .lp-gate: the lockup above, a pale wash and two dotted orbits behind. Those are sign-in and path choice in this repository.

Pinned light like the page it opens over, and scoped to .landing: the panel is light in both themes.

Behaviour on width

One container point, 40rem. Below it the dialog becomes a sheet that rises from the foot, the card loses width and padding, the orbit tightens, the two doors stack and their pictures scale to .78. The panel's height is measured by the component and written as a transition, so a taller step opens the card and a shorter one closes it; on this stand each step is drawn at its own height.

Limits

The dialog is not a route. A modal that owns a URL costs the root layout a parallel slot and gives a sign-in form a history entry nobody asked for (P17, rejected). Guards, expired links and the doors of node 1.2 land on the page form because they arrive by address.

The keyframes are this file's, because the orbit runs on its own clock, which a transition cannot. Under reduced motion base.css ends each at once: the tiles stand still at their declared places and every arrival is already arrived.

No divider reading "or" between the two buttons: no line owns it, and two stacked buttons of different weight already read as a choice (P17, rejected).
