How to read it

Answer a group at a time, not a row at a time. The six groups are sorted by what an answer costs rather than by where the row was found: the first two move no pixel at all, the third moves a few, and the last two are rounds of work. A group can be answered with one word if the recommendations hold, and a row that needs its own conversation is better spotted against the five beside it than alone in a 443-line file.

Where a recommendation says "close it", the work is a paragraph rather than a change. Seven of the twenty-four end that way: the measurement already answers the question and what is missing is somebody saying so out loud, in writing, so the next audit does not raise it again as new. That is not a formality here - two rows on this page were raised twice already by two different instruments, and one of them, the .muted size, was ANSWERED IN THE CODE on 2026-08-16 and nobody closed the row.

Every number on this page was re-measured on 2026-08-20 or names the corpus it was measured on. One row says so explicitly and it matters: the icon set's 22 and 106 per cent were counted over FOUR icons and there are five. That row's recommendation is therefore to re-measure before deciding, which is the only recommendation on this page that asks for work before an answer.

The icon set is optically unbalanced22 per cent between painted boxes and 106 per cent in INK, .ic-save against .ic-you. Measured on FOUR icons and the set is now fiveRedraw, leave it, or re-measure firstRE-MEASURED 2026-08-20 over all five, and the finding holds. The spread is 2.04x, 104 per cent, and the extremes are the same two. (An earlier run the same day read 100 per cent on a different alpha floor; the drawing did not move, the threshold did.) The element boxes are identical at 22 by 22, so the old "22 per cent" was about the drawn glyph's own bounding box and this measurement does not reproduce it. AND THE CAUSE IS NOT STROKE WEIGHT. All five are drawn at stroke-width: 1.7 on a 24 viewBox; what differs is PATH LENGTH. Two of the five are closed boxes - .ic-save is two full rectangles plus a divider, .ic-trends is one rectangle plus a curve - and a rectangle at this size carries roughly twice the perimeter of an open glyph like the person or the bell. So the honest choice is not "thin the heavy ones", it is whether this set is boxed or open, and thinning one icon's stroke to match would make the set look inconsistent at close range instead of at a glance. The ground is in the concept: optical evenness is what makes a row of five read as one object, and one object is calm. The drawing itself is the founder's and nothing in the product is broken while it waits
