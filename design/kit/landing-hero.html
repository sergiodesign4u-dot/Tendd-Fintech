Landing hero

The first screenful: the promise on the left and the product's own calm list on the right as the proof of it. Below the tablet point they stack in the order of the argument, claim first.

Organism 1 grey page 1 coloured page form: stacked / two columns added 2026-08-14

What it is

The proof is the product, not a picture of it. The panel is built from the components the app screens are built from - .card, .row, .logo, .chip, .amt - through the same stylesheet, so it cannot drift from what a person actually gets. A landing that draws its own mockup starts lying the first time a row changes.

And it carries no .total. The figure in the panel is an example and the markup says so. 46px belongs to a number a person owns, so the example wears .amt.figure at --type-figure instead, which is the step the share card's total takes for the same reason. The biggest thing on this page is the promise; the biggest thing inside the app is your money. The One Number Rule is kept rather than bent.

Anatomy

  • .k the eyebrow, "For people who are not into finance". The same atom every block mark in the product uses
  • .lp-h1 the one place --type-hero is spent. Tracked in, because at this size the default fitting opens up and a headline set loose reads as a poster rather than as a sentence somebody means
  • .lp-lead the promise in one sentence, at the reading measure
  • .actions the action row, the same one every screen carries. One filled, one outline
  • .lp-preview the proof panel: a label, a count, the example figure, its caption and three real rows with real merchant marks

Behaviour on width

One point, the registered 47.5rem, where claim and proof stand side by side at 55% and 45%. Not 50/50: equal columns would make the page read as a comparison between two things, and the claim leads.

Limits

The headline's measure is 14ch and not --container-text, and it is the one width this file sets. 52ch is a reading measure, for prose a person works through; a headline is read in one glance and wants to break where the sentence does. 14ch puts "See what you're paying for." on its own lines and "Calmly." alone underneath.

The headline is fluid, and it was not at first. The token was written as a flat 3.5rem with a comment refusing fluid on the ground that there was no content reason. There was: at a fixed 56px the headline ran to four lines at 390 and pushed the lead, both buttons and the whole proof panel below the fold. A headline that pushes its own call to action off the screen is not a headline. Measured after: 42px at 390, 52 at the tablet point, 56 from about 920 up.

Where it comes from

Drawn by design/system/components/landing-hero.css through ../system/index.css, the same entry point a product screen loads. It stands on the coloured landing and on wireframes/index.html, and on nothing else: the landing is the only public surface this product has. Registered in docs/inventory.md and in _nav.js, in the organism group.
