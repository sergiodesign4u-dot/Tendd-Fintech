Landing cut list

The second half of the public page's argument, and the only place on the whole surface where a number moves. Fourteen real rows, three real cancellations, and a total that comes down by exactly the three prices on the screen.

Organism 1 grey page 1 coloured page a measure, no point added 2026-08-14

What it is

It is the value, demonstrated rather than claimed. The page's promise is "see what you are paying for, cancel what you do not use, spend less". Written out, that is a marketing sentence a person who avoids finance apps has read a hundred times. Shown, it is fourteen real rows, three real cancellations, and arithmetic anybody can check against the prices in front of them. That is the whole difference this block exists to make, and it is why the drop is not a round number.

$192.90 becomes $144.92, and every part of that is the product's own. Netflix $17.99, which cancel-win cancels and frees exactly this much. The New York Times $17.00 and Peloton App $12.99, the two home-savefocus flags as not opened in weeks. Their two are also the two behind that screen's own line, "You could save up to $29.99 a month by cutting 2 you might not be using": 12.99 + 17.00 = 29.99. Nothing here was picked to look good.

The founder's sketch put the end figure at $79, which would be cutting $114 of a $192.90 stack. A 59% saving is a claim this product makes nowhere, and the label "Example, not your data" does not make a claim like that safe on the one surface whose job is to be trusted by somebody who already does not trust finance apps. Recorded, with the number that was used instead, in docs/decisions.md under D-Hero.

The rows are .row, the product's own. Not a landing lookalike. The mark, the name, the category, the amount and the chip are the same five slots Home puts in front of a person, through the same stylesheet, so this block cannot drift from what somebody actually gets.

Anatomy

  • .lp-cut the block: surface fill, container hairline, container radius, and the app's own --container-page measure. At the section's full 1200 a row is a 36px mark and a price with three hundred pixels of nothing between them, which reads as a table rather than as a calm list. No overflow anywhere in it, and that is load-bearing: an ancestor with overflow: hidden becomes the scrollport a sticky element sticks inside, which silently turns sticky back into static
  • .cuthead sticky, offset by the landing bar's own height so a second sticky element does not slide under the first. Written as --tap + --space-16 + 1px rather than as 61 so it moves when the bar does
  • .cutstack four states in one grid cell. A counter that interpolates cannot say $174.91: counter() renders an integer and there is nowhere to put the decimal point. All four totals and all four counts are real text in the page, and the scroll cross-fades them. It is exact to the cent, and every figure a person can see is a string microcopy.md owns
  • .chipswap two chips, both real text. CSS cannot rewrite the words inside an element, and a content string on a pseudo would put a line of product copy somewhere the voice inventory cannot own it
  • .row.cut1 / .cut2 / .cut3 the three moments, named in the markup. The order is a class rather than a position: :nth-of-type counts elements of the same tag among their siblings, and every row is the only child of its own li, so all three were number one and all three cancelled at once

Behaviour on width

A measure and no point. --container-page caps the block and centres it, and the rows inside are the product's own rows, which already know what to do with a narrow one. No query in this file.

Limits

Not one animation shorthand in the scroll block. The shorthand resets animation-range and animation-timeline to their initial values, so a shorthand written after a range silently erases it. Longhands only.

Every moment rule is written at the same specificity as the rule it is timing, and after it. The first draft timed the chips with .row.cut1 .chipswap > * at 0-5-0 against a .row.cut .chipswap > .live at 0-6-0, lost the cascade, and every chip animated across the entire range instead of at its moment: all three cancellations happened at once and it looked like one transition. Found by scrubbing the block at eleven scroll positions, not by looking at it.

Three keyframe sets, and the middle one is why there are three. A state that only arrives is wshow; one that only leaves is wfade; the two middle totals do both, and stacking two animations on one element to get that does not work. Two animations on one property are resolved by list order, and an animation with fill-mode: both is applying a value the whole time, before and after its own range, so whichever of the pair is later wins even when the other is the one that should be speaking. The two edges have to live in one animation, which is what the percentage stops in wswap are: it is handed a range spanning both moments, and 27% and 73% of it are the two moments.

The timeline is named and declared on the block, not taken with a bare view() on each row. A bare view() measures each element's own box, and with fourteen rows down a tall block that is fourteen different timelines: the last row would cancel before the first, because it enters the screen later and its own progress runs on its own clock.

Behind @supports, and the base state is the honest one. Unguarded, everything falls back to the document timeline and all three cancellations plus the whole fall play once on load, before the block is near the screen. Guarded, such a browser renders fourteen live rows, $192.90 and "14 subscriptions": a true and complete picture of the calm view with nothing missing but the demonstration. It is the same state prefers-reduced-motion is given, and every string of the story is still in the page for anybody reading it with something other than their eyes.

A cancelled row does not turn red and it is not struck through. It goes quiet: the row steps back to the recessed ground. Principle 1 governs this even in a demonstration - cancelling is a small win, not an alarm.
