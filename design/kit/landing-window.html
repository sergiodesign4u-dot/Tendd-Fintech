Landing window

The hero's proof: under the example's total and its count, a matrix of the person's own apps, drifting. Three bands, and they do not travel the same way.

Organism 1 grey page 1 coloured page does not adapt added 2026-08-14

What it is

It is an argument, not an ornament. A still list is a screenshot, and a person who has never felt the problem reads a screenshot as somebody else's tidy spreadsheet. The drift says the part a screenshot cannot: that a subscription stack is not a list somebody sat down and made, it arrives, one charge at a time, from more directions than anybody is watching.

Principle 1 governs screens, and this is the one public surface. "Every screen lowers anxiety" is a rule about the product a person is inside. Here is where the anxiety somebody already has is named back to them once, calmly, before the promise. No red, no urgency, nothing flashing: marks moving slower than reading.

The number does not move here. It is stated at --type-figure with its label and its caption, and it stays $192.90 for as long as anybody looks at it. The one place it moves is the cut list, where three subscriptions are visibly cancelled and the drop is the sum of the three prices on the screen. A number that changes for a reason a person can check is a demonstration; a number that changes on its own is a claim. The first build of this component had it climb 192 to 199 as a watermark behind the tiles, and that was the wrong instinct: it made the page argue with the figure printed two lines above it.

A square, not a row. Each tile is a mark with its price under it, because the matrix has to read as a field of things rather than as a list. The list is what the product gives you in the next section, and the whole hand-off depends on those being two different shapes. The square is sized in cqw, so about three stand across the opening whether it is 286 wide or 518.

Anatomy

  • .lp-window the opening: --bg-recessed, one step under the card it is cut into, so a tile reads as an object lying on something. overflow: hidden is the whole component: it makes a track wider than the page cost nothing, and it is why no width question is ever asked about a track
  • .wrows the stack of three, and the one place the edge fade is declared: a mask on the stack rather than on each band, so all three dissolve on the same two edges
  • .wtrack the same tiles twice, travelling exactly half its own width. That is what makes the loop seamless, and it is why the gap between tiles is a margin-right and not a gap: with gap there are 2n-1 gaps for 2n tiles, half the width falls half a gap short of one set, and the band jumps 4px once a cycle
  • .wtile a square, a mark and a price. aspect-ratio: 1 and not a height, so it stays square while its width is a ratio of the opening. The mark is .logo at its base 36px and the price is .amt at its base size, neither given a rule by this file

Behaviour on width

No point and no query. The square is a ratio of the opening's own width in cqw, clamped at both ends, so three of them stand across it at every width the product has. The number of tiles in a band is written nowhere.

Limits

The fourteen are the canonical dataset, not a sample written here. voice/docs/microcopy.md has carried the fourteen subscriptions, their categories and their prices under "Canonical subscription dataset" since the voice stage, summing to $192.90. That file is the owner. The first build of this component invented nine of the fourteen prices to close the total, believing no itemisation existed, and shipped three that contradicted the fixtures: Disney+ at 9.99 against 13.99, Hulu at 9.99 against 7.99, Notion at 10.00 against 8.00. Corrected 2026-08-14. The script that writes the markup now reads the fixture table line by line and refuses to write anything that disagrees with it.

overflow: hidden clips paint, not measurement, and that cost the grey corpus 204px of sideways scroll before it was caught. A track is far wider than its opening; hiding the overflow stops it being drawn outside and does not stop an ancestor from measuring it. In colour this is harmless, because landing-hero.css gives the panel min-width: 0 past the point and below it the panel is a stretched column flex item. In the grey the same panel is a grid item, whose automatic minimum is its content's min-content size.

The hand-off is a reading, not a translation. The founder asked that the subscriptions travel with the reader into the next section. Nothing in CSS carries an element out of one section and into another, so what is built is the perception: as the hero leaves, the field sinks and fades, and the cut list lifts its rows into the same place at the same moment. The range is exit and not cover, because exit is "the opening is leaving the top of the screen", which is the same moment on a phone and on a desktop; a cover percentage is not.

The hand-off is behind @supports. Unguarded, animation-timeline degrades in the worst possible way: the declarations stay valid, the animation falls back to the document timeline, and the field sinks and disappears once on load at the top of the page. Guarded, such a browser renders the field, drifting, with nothing missing but the hand-off.

It changed a frozen file, and that is a founder's decision. Written into wireframes/index.html at the block it changes and recorded as D-Hero in docs/decisions.md. In the grey the same fourteen squares stand still: the grey is a structure contract, and motion is not structure.

Where it comes from

Drawn by design/system/components/landing-window.css through ../system/index.css, the same entry point a product screen loads, and placed by landing-hero.css, which owns .lp-preview and therefore owns where the opening is cut into it. It stands on the coloured landing and on wireframes/index.html, and on nothing else. Registered in docs/inventory.md and in _nav.js, in the organism group.
