Landing window

One opening, two forms and one argument. In the hero it is cut into the proof panel and fourteen merchants drift past a number that climbs. At the foot of "How Tendd works" the same fourteen stand still in one grid, one of them leaves, and the number goes down. The only moving thing in this product that is not a loading state.

What it is

It is an argument, not an ornament. The panel above it states the example in words and in one figure, and a person who has never felt the problem reads that as a screenshot. This says the part a screenshot cannot: that a subscription stack is not a list somebody sat down and made, it arrives, one charge at a time, from fourteen directions, while the total goes up on its own and nobody is watching. The drift is the arriving. The climbing number is the not watching.

Principle 1 governs screens, and this is the one public surface. "Every screen lowers anxiety" is a rule about the product a person is inside. Here is where the anxiety somebody already has is named back to them once, calmly, before the promise. No red, no urgency, nothing flashing: a grey number moving a dollar every ten seconds.

It is the mark at hero scale. D-Brand locks the identity as Crop: one letterform larger than any frame, and a window cut out of it. The figure is set to the width of the opening and past it; what does the cutting is first the frame and then the tiles themselves, which pass in front of it and take it away a piece at a time.

And the example finally adds up. Before this opening the panel claimed "14 subscriptions" and "$192.90" and showed three rows: the other eleven were an assertion and the total was a number with nothing under it. The fourteen tiles are the product's own fourteen merchants, and their prices sum to $192.90 to the cent. Five already carried a price in the corpus (Netflix 17.99, Spotify Premium 11.99, Peloton App 12.99, ChatGPT Plus 20.00, The New York Times 17.00 = 79.97); the other nine were chosen to close the remaining 112.93 exactly, and the script that writes the markup refuses to write it if they do not.

Variants

  • base the hero's opening, cut into .lp-preview. Three tracks, each carrying its tiles twice and travelling exactly half its own width. The figure climbs 192 to 199 on the document's clock, a dollar every ten seconds. This is the problem: charges arriving from three directions past a number that goes up on its own
  • .gather the same component saying the opposite thing, at the foot of "How Tendd works". Nothing drifts; the fourteen stand in one auto-fit grid, which is the calm view this product sells. The figure falls 192 to 174, and it is driven by the scroll rather than by a clock, so it does not happen at a person: it happens because they moved
  • .wtile.cut the one that leaves. It gathers with the rest and then goes, leaving its cell empty rather than closing the gap: a grid that reflowed would say there were always thirteen, and the hole is the point

The variant carries no copy of its own and needs none. Step 3 of the section it closes already reads "Feel the small win when the number goes down." The sentence was written at the wireframes; this is what it looks like.

192 to 174, and neither number is invented. The example totals $192.90. The product's own small-win screen cancels Netflix and frees $17.99 a month. 192.90 minus 17.99 is 174.91, which truncates to 174 exactly as 192.90 truncates to the 192 the hero starts on, and the tile marked .cut is the same Netflix. The landing does not get a story of its own.

What the variant actually costs is the test of whether it is one. A grid instead of three tracks, a border and a corner instead of a cut edge, and two animations on a view timeline. The ground, the figure's size and its anchoring at each end of the range, the tile, the mark and the price are the base rules, unchanged.

Anatomy

  • .lp-window the opening: --bg-recessed, one step under the card it is cut into, so a tile reads as an object lying on something. overflow: hidden is the whole component - it crops the figure, it makes a track wider than the page cost nothing, and it is why no width question is ever asked about a track
  • .wfig the climbing figure, absolutely positioned behind everything, aria-hidden with its opening. Sized in cqw against the opening's own width, painted in --text-ghost, tabular so 192 and 199 are the same width and the numeral does not shift on the tick
  • .wrows the stack of three, and the one place the edge fade is declared: a mask on the stack rather than on each row, so all three dissolve on the same two edges and the figure behind them does not dissolve at all
  • .wtrack the same tiles twice, travelling exactly half its own width. That is what makes the loop seamless, and it is why the gap between tiles is a margin-right and not a gap: with gap there are 2n-1 gaps for 2n tiles, half the width falls half a gap short of one set, and the row jumps 4px once a cycle
  • .wgrid the gathered form's container: repeat(auto-fit, minmax(min(7.5rem, 100%), 1fr)), so the column count is written nowhere. Nine at a 1200 measure, five at the tablet point, two on a phone, and the day this product has a fifteenth merchant nothing here changes. The floor is wrapped in min() for the reason grid.css was corrected the same day: a floor taller than its container makes a track wider than the container
  • .wtile a mark and a price. The mark is .logo at the base 36px and the price is .amt at its base size, neither of them given a rule by this file: this component declares the box the two of them stand in and nothing about either. In a track it carries its own trailing gap as a margin; in the grid the gap is the grid's

Behaviour on width

One point, the registered 47.5rem, and the reason is arithmetic rather than taste. The three tracks are 178px tall at every width, because a mark and a price do not grow on a wider screen, while the figure grows with the opening. Centring the two on the same axis is therefore worst at the phone end: measured on a 390 screen the figure stood 181 tall against 178 of tracks and the tracks covered all but a hair of it. Below the point the two are anchored to opposite edges - the figure to the top, the tracks to the bottom, and the opening's 80px of top padding is the band the figure is seen in. Past the point the opening is 518 wide, the figure is 234 tall against 178 of tracks, and centring gives 28px clear above and below.

Limits

The figure is not a readout and must never become one. $192.90 is stated once, in text, above the opening, with its label and its caption. This numeral carries no cents, is cropped by its frame, sits at the ground's own value and is inside an aria-hidden element. It is a picture of a number. Principle 4 is why the honest figure stays in the panel: a number that moves is a number a person cannot check.

The climb is seven dollars over seventy seconds, and the first draft was wrong. It ran 192 to 216 in a minute. A background numeral that has drifted to 216 while the figure two lines above says $192.90 is a page arguing with itself, on the one surface whose whole job is to be trusted by somebody who does not trust finance apps. One dollar every ten seconds keeps the climb, stays within a few dollars of the stated figure for as long as anybody is looking, and never crosses 200 - which would read as a different number rather than the same one growing.

The scroll act is behind @supports, and leaving it unguarded would have been the worst kind of failure. Without animation-timeline the declarations stay valid and the animations fall back to the document timeline: the whole act would play itself once on load, at the top of the page, where nobody is looking. Guarded, such a browser renders the base state - fourteen tiles settled in the grid and the figure holding at 192 - which is a complete picture of the calm view with nothing missing from it but the movement. That is the same state prefers-reduced-motion is given.

The timeline is named and declared on the opening, not taken with a bare view() on each tile. A bare view() measures each tile's own box, and each tile's box is the thing being transformed: the animation would be reading a number it is writing. One name, one measured box, every child reading it.

Every range bound is a cover percentage read off the geometry, and it was wrong twice. The first draft ended the fall at cover 78%, which puts the opening's top 6px from the viewport top: the number finished falling as the block left. The second ended at 68% and was right on a desktop and wrong on a phone - past the point the figure is centred and is very nearly the whole block, so if the block is on screen the figure is; below the point it hangs in the opening's 80px of top padding while the block is 524 tall, so it leaves the screen long before the block does. Ending at cover 52% puts the opening's top at 304px on a 900 window and 133 on an 844 one. And no bound mixes entry with cover: entry is measured against the element's own height, and this element is 246px tall on a desktop and 524 on a phone, so the same entry 25% is two completely different places on the screen.

No JavaScript, and no library. The count is a registered custom property: @property --wfig-n typed as <integer>, animated, read back through counter-reset. Typing it is what makes it interpolate, and interpolating an integer is the tick - there is no keyframe per step and no steps() function. Where @property is not honoured the registration drops, the declaration survives as a plain custom property, and the opening renders the true 192 standing still.

Under prefers-reduced-motion the static state is the honest one, and it needed a line of its own. base.css already collapses every animation in the product, and for the tracks that is enough: they carry no fill-mode, so they return to translate3d(0,0,0), a full row of tiles standing still. The figure is the one animation in this system that ends forwards, and a forwards animation collapsed to nothing lands on its last keyframe: 199, a number that was never true. The component turns its own animation off instead, and the property returns to its declared 192.

The container-type is declared here, and that is the rule rather than an exception to it. It belongs to whoever places a component, and this opening places the figure. The figure is sized from the opening's own width and not the page's: the opening is 286px wide on a 320 screen and 518 in the hero's right column at 1280, two numbers a viewport unit cannot tell apart. Checked before declaring it - nothing inside the opening asks an @container question, so nothing changes its answer by being re-parented.

overflow: hidden clips paint, not measurement, and that cost the grey corpus 204px of sideways scroll before it was caught. A track is 1122px wide; hiding the overflow stops it being drawn outside the opening and does not stop an ancestor from measuring it. In colour this is harmless: landing-hero.css gives the panel min-width: 0 past the point, and below it the panel is a stretched column flex item whose width comes from its parent. In the grey the same panel is a grid item, and a grid item's automatic minimum is its content's min-content size, so the hero's column resolved to the track. The grey now carries min-width: 0; nothing was added to the colour, because a guard it does not need is a dead declaration.

It changed a frozen file, and that is a founder's decision rather than a build choice. The panel's three sample rows were structure, and structure lives in wireframes/. The decision is written into wireframes/index.html at the block it changes, and recorded as D-Hero in docs/decisions.md. In the grey the same fourteen tiles stand still: the grey is a structure contract, and motion is not structure.

Where it comes from

Drawn by design/system/components/landing-window.css through ../system/index.css, the same entry point a product screen loads, and placed by landing-hero.css, which owns .lp-preview and therefore owns where the opening is cut into it. It stands on the coloured landing and on wireframes/index.html, and on nothing else: the landing is the only public surface this product has. Registered in docs/inventory.md and in _nav.js, in the organism group. Its one token, --text-ghost, is in tokens.css with the measurement that put it there.
