Stage 11 · Animation · step 1 of the stage

Motion

One surface of this product moves a great deal and the other fifty-four do not move at all. That is the whole finding of the census below, and it is why this stage opens by counting rather than by designing: a scale derived from the one page that has motion would be a scale derived from a sample of one.

A moving page is also the only artefact in this system a screenshot cannot audit. A still frame of a scroll-driven stage and a still frame of a broken one look the same, so everything on this page was measured by script over the source, with comments stripped before anything was counted. This file's own predecessors have twice reported a defect that turned out to be a sentence of prose.

Four curves, named by job, readable from nowhere

landing-orbit.css declares them inside @supports, on .landing .lp-orbit.lp-story.fromcircle .storypin. They came out of the founder’s note of 2026-08-15 on a stage that was already doing everything it had been asked to: make it soft. Every animation on that page ran on linear, which on a scroll timeline is not a neutral default but the strongest decision in the file: it welds the object to the scrollbar, so nothing accelerates, nothing arrives and nothing settles.

CurveValueIts job
--ease-arrivecubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)expo-out. Covers the distance early and spends the rest closing the last few pixels, which is what an object landing looks like. Everything that comes ON uses it
--ease-leavecubic-bezier(0.6, 0, 0.85, 0.2)its mirror: slow to let go, then quick. Everything that goes OFF uses it, and an exit is always shorter than an entrance
--ease-settlelinear(), one overshoot of 1.5% at 66a spring. Used in exactly one place, the deck closing over a cancelled plate, because that is the one moment on the stage where something should feel like an object arriving at a stop rather than a value being interpolated
--ease-travelcubic-bezier(0.45, 0, 0.22, 1)one journey only: the two figures crossing most of a viewport, ten times any other travel on the stage. Expo-out over 600px is a rocket that then creeps

The drift has already happened, and it is four files wide

A custom property declared on one component’s pin cannot be read from another component. So landing-facts.css, landing-paths.css, landing-plan.css and landing-steps.css each write cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1) by hand for their own entrance. Five files, one curve, one name and four literals. That is not the argument for a token in the abstract: it is the drift the token exists to stop, already in the code. The orbit file predicted it in a comment and named this stage as the place it gets fixed.

The first two groups are deliberately mutually prime so that nothing on the stage ever loops in step with anything else, and they are not candidates for a scale: a loop length is not a duration in the sense a scale means it. The scale this stage owes is the one that does not exist yet, because the product has no transitions to time.

The other fifty-four screens: 68 state changes, every one a hard cut

The 26 include every control a person actually touches: button, switch-row, checkbox, select, text-input, tab-bar, nav-row, subscription-row, plan-option, preset-tile, door, panel, faq-list, app-bar. Nothing here is broken. An instant state change is a legitimate choice, and for a product whose first principle is calm it is a defensible one. What it is not is a recorded choice: no file says “this changes instantly, and here is why”, so today it cannot be told apart from an omission.

The bottom sheet is the loudest case. dialog-sheet.css is the product’s only consumer of --shadow and it carries the three moments where this product asks a person to decide something: “Add a source”, “Cancelling can be made deliberately hard, and it is not your fault”, “Delete everything Tendd holds for you?”. It appears with no travel at all.

The guard is correct, and it currently guards nothing

base.css carries the product-wide prefers-reduced-motion block: animation-duration: 0.01ms, animation-iteration-count: 1, transition-duration: 0.01ms, scroll-behavior: auto, all !important, on *. Its transition half reaches zero declarations, because there are none. It becomes load-bearing on the first line this stage writes, which is the right order: the guard was put in before the thing it guards, and it was put in because it had once been written at the wrong specificity and every skeleton pulsed forever for a person who had asked the operating system for stillness.

Eight further prefers-reduced-motion blocks live in the six landing files and one more in design/story.js. Nine motion guards for one page, one for the other fifty-four.

One dead keyframe, found by the count and deleted the same day

@keyframes srise in landing-story.css: defined, and named by nothing. It was the story head’s entrance until that file recorded the decision that the head does not make one, and the reason is written beside the rule that took it off: at contain 0%, the first frame anybody sees when the stage locks, its fill-mode held the heading at opacity 0 and the reader was handed fourteen unlabelled squares with no heading, no number and no reason. The reference went. The four lines of the keyframe stayed.

Same class of leftover as the centred stage deleted the day before and one order of magnitude smaller, found the same way: by reading every @keyframes in the system against every animation-name in it. 31 defined, 31 named after the deletion. The check is part of the census script now rather than a thing somebody remembers to run.

What the census leaves to decide

Four questions, none of them answerable by measuring, which is why they are listed here rather than settled here.

#The questionWhat the census contributes
1Do the four curves become tokens, and at which level?They are values, they are already named by job rather than by shape, and one of them is copied by hand into four files. The two-level rule gives the shape: --ease-expo-out as the primitive, --ease-arrive as the role. The open half is whether four is the right number for a product whose motion is about to double
2Does the app get motion at all, and what kind?26 components change state instantly. Three answers are all defensible: leave it and write down why; give the system one transition on the two properties that carry a state change, colour and border; or design it per component. The founder’s call, and the biggest in the stage, because it is the only one that touches every screen
3What happens between two states that are documented as two pages?The product has 7 loading pages, 7 empty pages and 7 error pages. Every state change it documents is documented as a separate file, so the movement between them has no owner anywhere in this repo. Either the stage names that movement, or it says plainly that a state change here is a page change and carries none
4Is the landing’s language the product’s language?Arrive and leave, an exit shorter than its entrance, and a curve for a long journey are a complete and coherent vocabulary. It was derived on one surface, and rule 4 of this system exists precisely because a scale derived from a sample of one is not a scale

What this page does not have yet, and why. There is no live curve stand on it. A stand that lets you run the four curves against each other is worth building and it belongs to the step that decides which curves survive: built now it would specimen four values that question 1 may still change, and a specimen of a value that moves is how a stand starts lying.

The full census, the audit and the four open questions are in docs/motion.md. The curves live for now in design/system/components/landing-orbit.css, scoped to one selector, and this stage is where they leave it. The page they were written for is the landing; the guard that will hold them is in design/system/base.css; the rule that decides where a value is allowed to live is in Architecture.
