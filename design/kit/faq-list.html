What it is

That is not a technical preference. ia/docs/pages/ puts an FAQPage schema on this node, and a schema that describes text a crawler cannot read is a claim about a page that does not exist.

No JavaScript and no ARIA. <details> and <summary> already are a disclosure widget: they carry the expanded state, the keyboard behaviour and the announcement, and every attribute this file could add would repeat something the element does better.

Rebuilt 2026-08-16, and the rebuild is a desync repair rather than a new idea. The founder, with a reference: "давай делать ... сделай красиво". What the reference shows is a heading standing on the left, the questions as open rows on the page's own ground with a hairline between them, and a plus that becomes a minus. Two of those three are what the frozen grey has drawn since the wireframes: _wf.css gives this block a top border, a border under each question and a + that turns into a dash, with no card anywhere. The rollout put the four rows inside a white card and swapped the mark for a chevron, and nothing had asked for either. So the card is gone, the mark is a plus again, and the grey is the contract it always was. What is genuinely new is the split, and the first answer standing open.

Anatomy

  • .lp-faq no container at all since 2026-08-16. A top border and a border under each question, both --line-container, and the row is its own boundary. Four questions in a white box on a canvas band read as one object a person has to open; four rows on the band read as four things, which is what they are
  • .lp-section:has(.lp-faq) > .lp-wrap the split, past the desktop point: the heading takes a column of its own and sticks while the list scrolls. Asked with :has() because the section carries no class and this is a fact about what it CONTAINS, so neither corpus had to learn a name
  • summary a control, so it takes the 44px floor and the system's one focus ring from base.css. The browser's own triangle is removed in both engines, because two marks pointing different ways is how a disclosure stops being readable
  • the mark a plus that becomes a minus, which is what the grey has drawn since the wireframes. Two gradient stripes in one 16px box, 1.5px in currentColor: no second element, no font glyph, no icon file, and the vertical stripe collapses to nothing when the answer is open. A chevron says WHICH WAY and is the same glyph nav-row.css uses for a link to another page; a plus says WHAT WILL HAPPEN, and on a block whose job is to answer without making a person leave the page, that difference is the block
  • the answer body ink at the reading measure. The top padding is zero because the question above already spent it: a gap on both sides of the seam reads as two blocks rather than a question and its answer

Behaviour on width

One column until the desktop point, two after it. Below --bp-desktop the heading sits over the list as it always did. Above it the wrap becomes a grid of 3 to 8, a fraction rather than a width, because the left column exists to hold four words and what matters is that they wrap into a block of type rather than that the column is any particular number of pixels: measured, it is 206px at the desktop point where the heading takes three lines and 310 at 1440 where it takes two. The heading is sticky at --space-96, which is the landing's sticky bar (73px, measured) plus air, and it needs align-self: start to be sticky at all - a stretched grid item is as tall as its row and has nothing to stick inside. The answer keeps the 52ch reading measure at every width, measured at 459px.

Limits

The first answer stands open, and that is a product decision rather than a decorative one. Closed, this block is four questions and no evidence that answers exist. The one it opens with is the most anxiety-reducing line on the page for the person this product is for: she arrives asking whether she has to hand over her bank, and the first word she reads here is "No." The other three stay closed, because a block that opens everything is a page of text and not a set of questions. It is a state change on a frozen page, so the founder's decision of 2026-08-16 is written on the section in wireframes/index.html.

The dividers are measured and they differ by theme, by the ground and not by a choice. --line-divider is the line inside a container and is tuned to sit on paper: on the page ground it is 1.01:1, which is not a faint line but no line at all, which is why this block reads --line-container instead. That is 1.09:1 here in the light theme and 1.46 in the dark, where the same pair on paper is 1.23 and 1.33. Neither owes a threshold - a divider is decorative and nothing in this block has to be found by touch.

No animation here, and it is left for stage 11 rather than improvised. A height transition on a disclosure needs either a fixed height, which the copy does not have, or interpolate-size, which is a decision about how this product moves and belongs to the stage that owns motion. An unfinished animation is worse than none: it is the one a person notices.
