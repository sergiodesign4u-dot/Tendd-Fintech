Share card

The picture a person sends to somebody else after a cancellation. It is the only centred thing in the product, and the only object built to be looked at outside the app. An image made of two numbers, which is what makes it safe by construction rather than safe by memory.

Molecule2 grey pages0 coloured1 formno states

Anatomy

Five slots, four of which are atoms, inside one card that is deliberately dumb: it has no behaviour, no target and no data path to anything except a count and a total. Everything below is drawn by design/system/components/share-card.css and the atom files it hosts, through the same entry point a product screen loads.

  • .sharecard the molecule: a div with role="img" and a full accessible name, the container hairline, the container corner and centred text. The role is what makes a screen reader read the card as one picture rather than as five orphaned fragments
  • .lbl the caption, the label atom in its quiet form. This file declares NOTHING about it: kit.css restated the atom's base rule here word for word, and the copy was deleted
  • .count the card's own slot: "13 subscriptions" at 20px, the second number and deliberately smaller than the first
  • .total the big total atom at its 32px share size, declared in big-total.css. It is the one place in the product where the total is not 46px, and that step down is the single named exception in the whole scale mapping
  • .p "a month, all in one calm place", the muted line atom. Its 4px top margin lives in muted-line.css
  • .win-line the card's other own slot: the sentence the card exists to say, separated by a divider rather than by a second card

Variants and sizes

One form. The inventory records no axis for this component and the browser sweep agrees: there is one rule, one size, one fill, one corner, and every share card in the product is the same object. What changes between the two pages it stands on is not the card but what has been put inside it, and the CSS does not know the difference.

When to use it

Once, at the one genuinely good moment this product has. A person has cancelled something they had forgotten they were paying for, and the design principle that covers it says so out loud: the moment a forgotten subscription is found is the product's most important emotional beat. The card is what lets them tell somebody. Everything about it is built so that telling somebody costs nothing: "Here is the card, exactly as it will look. Nothing is shared until you tap Share."

Safe by construction is the design, not a policy. There is no slot on this card for an account number, a bank name or a list of what a person pays for, so no bank data can reach it even by accident. The block under it names the three things that are on it, which is proof rather than a promise: "The number of subscriptions, the monthly total, and the one you just cancelled. No account numbers, no bank details, and no list of what you pay for."

Never make it a target
<a class="sharecard" href="..."> or <button class="sharecard">

The card is not the Share button. It is a preview, and a preview that can be tapped is a preview that will be tapped by somebody who meant to look at it first. The action stands underneath it in an action row, where "Share" is a filled button and "Back to your subscriptions" is an outline one, and the sentence above the card says the tap is the only thing that shares anything.

States

None, and that is a finding rather than a gap. The card is a div with role="img": no anchor, no button, no target of any kind anywhere inside it, on either of the two pages it stands on. It is never hovered, never focused, never pressed and never disabled. Drawing four cells would document something the product cannot do, and making it hoverable would be the anti-rule above. The controls that act on it are buttons in an action row underneath, and their four states are on button.html.

One rule was dropped and it is the only thing here that is not a straight carry. kit.css declared .sharecard .lbl with the same display, the same size and the same ink the Label atom already gives it, and it did not even override the atom's margin. A host repeating an atom is not a variant, it is a second copy that will not be updated when the first one is. Deleting it moves zero pixels, checked against label.css before it went.

Two rules that look like this file's are deliberately not in it. .sharecard .total lives in big-total.css, because a 40px total folding to 32px is the atom's own size axis and the one named exception in the scale mapping; .sharecard .p lives in muted-line.css, which offers it back in writing. A rule that belongs to two files ends up in neither, so it is left in one, and the day the split is revisited it moves in a single step rather than in two.
