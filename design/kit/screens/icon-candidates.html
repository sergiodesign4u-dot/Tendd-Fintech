Destination icons: four candidates, one decision

The tab bar is four marks in one 22px row. They are all drawn at stroke-width: 1.7 on the same 24 cell, so what makes them look uneven is not weight, it is path length: a closed box carries roughly twice the perimeter of an open glyph. Ink is the share of the 24 cell actually painted, rasterised at 40 samples per module with an alpha floor of 10. The spread on the right is heaviest over lightest.

C is the trap. "Just take the box off" is the obvious move and it makes the row worse, not better: the chart falls to 10.60 and becomes the lightest thing in the bar, so the spread goes from 81 per cent to 99. A mark needs enough structure to hold its own beside three neighbours.

E was the recommendation and B is what shipped. Founder, 2026-08-21: "давай B". One glyph changes and only by subtraction - the chart gives up its frame for an axis corner, 23.82 to 14.71 per cent of ink - and the house keeps its door. The spread is 61 per cent, down from 81, and the heaviest mark in the bar is now the house rather than the chart. E stays on record: if the row ever needs to come tighter, the door is the next thing to go, and it is one decision away.
