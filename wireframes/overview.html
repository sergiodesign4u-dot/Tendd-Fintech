Flows and their screens

Five flows from flows.md, each one a person doing one job. Flow A is the first flow and is built end to end; it ends at Home, which is the etalon. A screen appears once with its job and is marked as shared where a later flow reuses it. States live in the matrix below, not here: this section is the route.

Why Home is the etalon, and what it has to carry. Five of the seven global elements stand on it at once (GC1 the header, GC2 the tab bar, GC3 the summary strip, GC4 the row, GC6 the trust line), and the two that are missing are missing on purpose: GC5 belongs to Alerts, and GC7 is barred from the calm view by D3. An etalon is what later screens are measured against, so the screen that already contains most of the product is worth more than the one a person sees first. Ground: docs/decisions.md, 2026-08-10.

The strategic dimension, on three named elements. benchmark.md names one dimension and calls it an activation requirement rather than a nice-to-have: trust and first-time clarity. On Home it is three things you can point at, not a general feeling. GC4, the row: logo, real merchant name, amount, "in 2 days, Aug 3", with the raw statement string deliberately absent here and living on node 2.7 as the decoder line. The attention row: "Netflix went up by $2.50, now $17.99 a month", which says who did the thing rather than announcing that a change was detected. GC6, the trust line: "Read-only. Tendd cannot move your money", beside where the figures came from and when they were last checked. Written down on 2026-08-10: all three were built from the first day, the paragraph naming them was the check the stage owed.

A wait is not a dead end, and it does not get a button. An edge a person takes is a real control on the screen. A prototype has one person in it, and the states that belong to a different person are honest to draw and dishonest to link.

Conventions

The contract every screen and every subagent follows, from docs/conventions.md. It is shown here because a rule that lives only in a markdown file is a rule only the model reads.

0What this inherits, and from where

Nothing in the conventions is invented at this stage. Each line names its owner, and a gap is fixed in the owner first and rendered second.

A-E SEO copy exists for node 1.1 only, the one public indexed screen. Every other node is noindex and carries no A-E block by design, so the text of every app screen comes from the microcopy inventory. That is a deliberate difference from the generic instruction "take the text from the A-E block".

1A live screen, not a schema

  • Every page is a real screen at full viewport, mobile first, that scrolls and clicks.
  • No zone labels. If a zone needs a caption to be understood, the zone is wrong, and that is a finding for the IA, not a sticker.
  • No "main action" captions. The main action is a real button that goes somewhere.
  • No mockup frame. A screen is not a 420 by 720 card floating in a grey stage.
  • One screen per page, one viewport. Mobile is checked by narrowing the browser.
  • A loading state is a real screen with grey placeholders in the shape of the content.

2Grey, structure only

  • Greyscale from _wf.css. No colour, no brand, no accent.
  • No type decisions beyond the browser heading hierarchy.
  • No shadows, gradients, decorative radii, icons, images or logos. Where an asset will sit later, a labelled grey box.
  • Placeholders stand for a deferred asset, never for missing text. No lorem ipsum, no "Heading 1": the copy exists.

3Inline CSS is transport

  • At fan-out, parallel agents cannot all write into _wf.css, so each screen carries its CSS inline. That is temporary by construction.
  • A token value never lives inline, not even on one screen. A screen writes var(--...); a missing variable is reported as "variable, value, why", not added.
  • A rule on two or more screens moves into _wf.css, the same two-occurrence test stage 07 uses to decide what is a component.
  • Every inline block opens with /* INLINE: <screen> :: for consolidation into _wf.css */, so the parent finds them mechanically.
  • The etalon consolidated early, at Step 5. The moment node 2.6 had its four states the shell was a five-screen rule, so it sits in _wf.css scoped under .app: strictly additive while the July .phone frame still had pages. The last of them left it in round 2, and the frame was deleted the same day.

4Semantic HTML

  • Real elements, not a wall of divs. Interactive things are real: a button is a button, a link is an anchor, a field has a label.
  • Exactly one h1 per page; repeated objects are list items or articles.
  • The accessible names are written now, not later: one name per subscription row carrying the whole row, a status role on the alert banner, live regions on the states that announce. They cost nothing here and are expensive to retrofit.

5One canonical set of numbers

  • Emma. 14 subscriptions, $192.90 a month.
  • Sources: Chase 11 and added by you 3. Eleven plus three is fourteen on every screen that shows either number. The three are named, from 2026-08-10: Peloton App, The New York Times, Notion. The set said how many were typed and never which, and one of them had to be Peloton: it is shown mid-trial on four screens, and a trial that has not charged produces no transaction, so no bank could be the source of that date. No page ever claimed Peloton came from Chase, so naming it costs nothing and makes the row honest.
  • Groups: Streaming 4, Software 4, Music, Fitness and News 6.
  • The alert case: Netflix up by $2.50, now $17.99, which is also the subscription the cancel chain runs on.
  • The decode case: SPOTIFYAB STOCKHOLM to Spotify Premium. The failed payment: Amazon Prime, on Jul 20, at $14.99.
  • Save focus: Peloton App $12.99 and The New York Times $17.00, so it offers "up to $29.99 a month".
  • The win: $17.99 a month, $215.88 a year, running total $32.98 a month.

6File naming

  • Lowercase Latin, hyphenated, all in wireframes/.
  • <screen>.html is the base page and is the success state.
  • <screen>-<state>.html is one page per state, named after the state itself, not after the nearest system word. A name not in the matrix does not get created.
  • index.html is the product home page, the public Welcome landing. The hub is this page. The two are never swapped.

7States: one page each

  • Every state is its own page, same structure, different content in the zone that changed. No JavaScript toggle inside one file: the prototype navigates between states.
  • The four system states are the floor. Domain and transit states are first class and get their own page.
  • No dead ends. Every state has a visible exit that exists in flows.md and points at a page that is really here.

8Two navigations, and they do not mix

  • This page carries the roadmap from the root registry: the bridge back into the project.
  • Every screen carries the wireframe-only panel from _nav.js: cluster, screen with its node number, its states, an accordion open on the current screen only, and a quiet cross-link to the IA node it renders.
  • No thin state strip above a screen, no roadmap on a screen page, no wireframe tree here.
  • The product chrome, the header and the tab bar, is the only navigation inside a screen. The onboarding chain shows no tab bar, and that absence is deliberate.
  • No footer inside the app. The one footer in the set is on the Welcome landing, which is a marketing page.

9One screen that reflows

  • Mobile first, authored narrow, reflowing to desktop through _wf.css.
  • The reflow implements the IA delta, decided once: the tab bar becomes a left rail with the header folded into its top; Home becomes a dashboard, the total and the attention row side by side and the groups in balanced columns; onboarding stays a single column, just wider.
  • No second pane on Home (decided at the etalon). The width goes into showing more of the list, not into a preview of one row, and an action has one destination at every width.
  • Every screen is checked at 360px: no horizontal scroll, no clipped text, no overlap.

10 and 11Deferred, and scope discipline

  • Deferred to Design: colour, brand, typography, shadows, real icons and logos, imagery, motion, exact spacing, the finished look. Also whether Cancel Guide, Share Snapshot and Upgrade become modals on desktop.
  • Every screen traces to a node in the sitemap and a place in the flows. Every block traces to the node file, whose composition traces to the block bank. A block that seems missing is a question for the node, not an invention here.
  • English in the docs, product language on the screens. No em dash anywhere.
