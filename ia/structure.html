Information Architecture Detail layer Structure

Structure

Every node of the product, as chips. Global elements frame every screen; pages are the screens themselves. A chip opens the specification of that node: its blocks, its states, its transitions and its SEO block.

The chips are rendered from one registry, ia/_nav.js: a node added there appears here the same moment, with no page to re-sync by hand. Until each node has its own page, a chip opens the node inside its cluster specification.

Detail layer

Nodes

Node number, name, type and scope. The tree view of the same material is on Sitemap; the data behind both is docs/sitemap.md.

Detail layer

Block bank by page type

Where the composition of a screen comes from. Every other artifact here has a rule of origin: a research fact carries a source, a CJM emotion carries a quote or a ?, a color comes off the brand plate, a line comes from the microcopy inventory. The list of blocks on a page had none, so it was invented on the spot, and an invention is always the median of everything seen before: hero, filter, cards, pagination. That median is what a thin wireframe looks like. Full data: docs/blocks.md.

Collected by page type, not by node: 16 screens collapse into 7 types, so the expensive step runs 7 times instead of 23. Two sources answer two different questions: competitors say what holds a person inside our category, Refero says how a page of this type is built in best-in-class products outside it.

Source availability, stated out loud

The rule about two sources says what is not enough, not what to do when one half is physically unavailable. For Tendd the domain half exists for two types only; the rest of the product is behind a competitor login, and we do not log in.

Our order for node 1.1, mobile-first

  1. Header. Wordmark, "How it works", Sign in
  2. Hero. H1, one-sentence promise, primary CTA, and the line "No bank connection needed to start"
  3. Product preview. The calm list with real merchant names and one total
  4. How it works. Three numbered steps, the length of the path stated up front
  5. Two paths. Connect a bank read-only, or add it yourself with presets
  6. Trust. What we access, what we never do, read-only, delete any time

  8. FAQ. Four objections, in our voice
  9. Closing CTA. The same single action
  10. Marketing footer. Privacy first

Ten blocks from five sources, matching none of them: ReSubs has no product preview and no two-paths block, Rocket Money has no trust block and no honest plan line, Visitors and Cushion carry a comparison table we dropped, Vivid and Copperx have no FAQ answering a bank objection. The reassurance line, the how-it-works strip and the FAQ are craft borrowed from outside the category; the preset row is the one block taken from a direct competitor, and it is repurposed.

Type B. Onboarding step (nodes 1.2, 1.3, 1.4, 1.5)

Four screens, one type: each asks for exactly one thing and then gets out of the way. This is the chain the product is judged on, because it is where the avoider decides to stay or to close the tab, and where the riskiest assumption is won or lost.

The domain half is missing here. Every competitor puts these screens behind signup. Two real domain sources survive, and neither is a marketing page: Plaid Link, the component we actually embed under D5, whose flow is published at plaid.com/docs/link and is a hard constraint rather than an inspiration; and our own research, the growth zones and jobs that say what these screens must defuse.

  • Plaid Link, opened 2026-08-04: Link opens, the person picks their bank, is handed off to the bank's own OAuth, signs in there, chooses which accounts to share, comes back linked. Credential entry, MFA, error handling and the confirmation email all live inside Link. We do not design those screens. Screenshot: research/screens/blocks-plaid-link-flow.png.
  • Craft, Refero: Arcade and Care.com (a screen that is only a question and option cards), Rarible (a line of consequence per option), Fernand (escape hatch), Rox (permission lines, read against write), Stripe (what is needed, how long it takes), IFTTT (search plus a grid of logo tiles), Square (the manual form), Quizlet and Product Hunt (a finished state that reports a result).

Type C. List dashboard (node 2.6 Home)

The screen the whole product is. Everything before it is a path to it, everything after it is a detail of it. It carries J-MAIN in one frame: all recurring charges in one calm view.

Here the domain half came back. Refero has the exact screen we could not open: Monarch Money's own recurring list, in a filled and an empty state, plus two subscription trackers on iOS. Second-hand captures, marked as such in every row.

  • Domain, second-hand through Refero: Monarch Money, recurring/all (a soft competitor in our own analysis) and the same screen empty; Subo and Orbit, category peers on iOS.
  • Craft, outside the category: Cursor billing (summary cards above a plain list, no chart anywhere), Dime (mobile: summary on top, grouped list under it), Teal (a list with exactly one primary action).

What Monarch's screen is made of: a sidebar of ten product sections with Recurring as one of them / three tabs / a filter row with a "Manage recurring" button / a five column table (name, next due date with days remaining in parentheses, payment account, category emoji, amount) / a free trial countdown and a discount offer pinned in the sidebar. Its empty state offers exactly one way out: "Connect an account to get started".

Type D. Object detail (node 2.7 Subscription Detail)

One subscription, everything known about it. This is where J3 lives, the clarity job: "what IS this charge". It is also the doorway to J2, the cut job, because a person decides to cancel here and not on the list.

Second-hand again, and this time from both sides of the mirror. Orbit has the detail sheet. Duolingo has the screen our person meets at the other end, when they go to the merchant to cancel: that one is a retention screen, and knowing its shape is what makes the cancel guide honest later.

  • Domain, second-hand through Refero: Orbit's subscription detail - hero with logo, name and price / five label and value rows (Billing, Next payment, Total spent, Subscribed, Category) / a Billing History card / a full width "Mark as Cancelled" / a "Delete subscription" link. Type E. Activity feed (node 3.8 Alerts)

The screen that closes J4, never be surprised by a price change or a failed payment. It is also the screen that decides whether the product is a source of calm or one more thing that buzzes at you.

Two findings before any block. On the domain side, the category has no such screen: Subo and Orbit configure reminders per subscription inside the add and edit flow and deliver them outside the app, so there is nowhere to go and see what happened. Rocket Money sells the promise on its public landing ("We'll notify you of important events that need your attention so you're never caught off guard") and exposes nothing behind it. On the craft side, the pattern exists everywhere, but the good examples are enterprise audit logs, whose register is the opposite of ours. Both findings push the same way: this screen has to be invented rather than adapted.

  • Domain: Rocket Money opened 2026-08-04, the promise in marketing with no screen behind it; Subo's add and edit and its reminder step, second-hand: a renewal reminder toggle, reminder timing and a notes field, all per subscription.
  • Craft: Dropbox admin activity log (search, filters, a multi column audit table), Airbnb notifications (one card: icon, message, dismiss), Missive (a feed inside a working surface).

The bank, closed

Seven types, thirteen MVP screens, and the composition of every one of them now has a source or a written reason. 114 block decisions: 78 taken, 34 dropped with the reason written down, 2 carried to the founder.

  • The availability table was wrong when it was written and is now right. "Behind login" was true of Playwright and false of the question, so five of the seven types got a domain half after all, marked second-hand.
  • Three types produced a live domain source: A (three competitor landings), B (Plaid Link, the component we embed), F (the ReSubs cancel guide library).
  • One type has no domain source anywhere, and that is the finding: E, because the category has no activity feed at all, only per-item reminders delivered outside the app.
  • The blocks that carry each screen are mostly the ones no source had: the decoder line at 2.7, the "needs you" split at 3.8, the price change that shows the old number, the last successful check at 6.14, the two paths in every empty state, and the trust line that appears on four screens.
  • One contradiction surfaced between the scope table and the line inventory (share on the MVP win screen against node 4.11 being LATER) and one assumption was flagged: which export is free. Both went to the founder rather than being resolved quietly, and both were answered on 2026-08-04 as D-Share and D-Export.

What this is for: the composition of a screen is no longer invented at wireframe time. Node specifications take their block list from here, and a block that is not in the bank and traces to nothing does not get added silently.
