The package boundary: what this is, and what it is not

This is a clickable product, not an application. There is no data layer, no API, no router, no persistence and no session. Nothing here talks to a bank, charges a card or remembers anything between two page loads. A reviewer can walk every flow end to end and a developer can read every rule, and neither of those is running software.

Every STATE is its own file. 17 screens carry 38 states between them and that is 57 html files: home.html, home-loading.html, home-empty.html, home-error.html, home-few.html, home-one.html are one screen. A real build collapses those 57 files back into 17 screens with states, and nothing in this repository says who owns that translation - it is the first decision a receiver makes and it is named here because it was written down nowhere.

What is real and ships as it standsWhat is not here at all
The design system. css that is production code, lifted and measured. See the lift test belowA backend. No database, no auth, no Plaid, no Stripe, no telemetry. The stack in CLAUDE.md is a hypothesis and nothing here depends on it
The copy. Every string on every screen is final and owned by voice/docs/microcopy.md, which is where a new one comes fromState. A filter does not filter, a form does not submit, a switch does not persist. The one exception is the trend chart's cursor
The structure. One IA node per screen, one flow per journey, and the behaviour spec that joins themA router. Navigation is an anchor from one file to another. What that becomes is the receiver's decision
The behaviour. Steps, states, edge cases and validation, every row naming its sourceAnything out of MVP. Budgeting, investments, a native app, bill negotiation, bill pay, a household view. Named in CLAUDE.md

Two scripts exist and they are not the same kind of thing. design/system/behaviour.js belongs to the system, is the only javascript it owns, travels with it, and draws the trend chart's cursor: 7 of the 57 screens load it. design/story.js belongs to the landing and drives its scroll choreography: 1 screen loads it. Everything else on every screen is html and css.

Which theme is the main one

Light is the main one, and dark is complete. The light palette is the one written on :root; the dark palette is written on [data-theme="dark"] and overrides it role by role. Every semantic role in the system exists in both or it does not exist at all, which is a rule of stage 08 and is checked rather than promised.

The switch you see on a screen is not part of the product. It belongs to design/_screen.css, the reviewer's chrome, together with the panel that names the screen and its IA node. Chrome is not shipped, and the map of the product deliberately does not include it.

So dark is a system capability that is fully paid for and a product feature that has never been specified. The product has no theme control and no string for one: Settings does not offer it and microcopy.md has no line for it. design/system/CLAUDE.md says the theme is set "however the product decides to set it" and nothing decides it. A clean-context reader asked this and could not answer it from any file, which is why it is written here. Whether the product ships a theme control, follows the operating system, or stays light-only is a decision and it is in the list further down.

A screen shows a state. What it does not show is what leads to that state, what happens at the edges, and what a field will refuse. That is handoff/docs/behaviour.md, and the thing that makes it a specification rather than a plausible description is the source column: every row names the file it was read from, and a row with no source is not written into the spec at all. It goes to a list addressed to a person.

The roll-call closes at five. Flows in ia/docs/flows.md = described 5 + deliberately not 0. The five sources are the screen file, the flow, the IA node, the figures document, and the grey wireframe - and the last of them is used zero times in all five flows, which is the honest version of "the grey is not a source of truth": it was available and it was never needed.

Nine states cannot be reached by clicking, and every one of them is right. The click graph reports them, and the spec answers each with the real trigger a build will fire: a bank connection that goes stale, a merchant with no cancellation guide, a person who has never had an account, a renewal that fails on a card that was working. The largest single reason is that the canonical person in this package is on Free, so the two screens that only a paying account ever sees have nothing to link them.

What each screen is made of

The inversion is two knees deep and that is the whole of why it works. A component reads colour only through a semantic role, so a flat inversion would name no primitive at all and the entire primitive level would report as unused. The chain followed is component to semantic role to primitive, in both themes, since a dark role points at a dark primitive.

The roll-call closes and the idle control is a number rather than an impression. Screens named by the registry 57 = mapped 57, with nothing on disk that the registry does not name and nothing named that is not on disk. Zero components stand on no screen, asked here for the third time and against the whole product rather than a sample. Three tokens are reached by no chain, and all three are accounted for: two are the width points, which a container query cannot read through a var(), so the token holds the decision and the literal in the query is its application; the third was declared idle and kept, with the date, in the file that declares it.

Two rows are real failures and neither was fixed here. Nine screens scroll sideways when text is set to twice its default size, all of them on a phone and none at desktop width; and the trend chart is made focusable at runtime, is not a native control, and therefore wears the browser's own focus ring rather than the system's. Both went to the backlog as debts with their measurements, because the product was accepted at stage 12 and an edit now would void every pixel comparison it stands on.

The zoom failure is the width sweep's own lesson arriving one axis later. Stage 10 swept 58 widths by moving the viewport and holding the text. A person with low vision does the opposite: they hold the viewport and move the text. Nobody had ever asked the question in that direction, and the answer is different.

And the instrument had to be made deterministic before any of its numbers could be believed. Its first two full runs reported catastrophic contrast failures, sixteen in one and twenty-eight in the next, on different screens each time, and none of them reproduced in isolation. The cause is in the system: every interactive element transitions its colours over the state duration, so asserting a theme starts a cross-fade across the whole page and a sample taken inside it compares one theme's ink against a surface halfway to the other. The contrast and focus passes now run with reduced motion emulated, which makes the product's own reduce block zero every duration; the motion pass needs a context of its own, since measuring the baseline inside a reduced one would report the reduce block as the product's motion, which is a check that always passes and proves nothing. Two consecutive runs now return byte-identical output, and that determinism is part of what "confirmed" means.

Every url() inside the folder resolves inside it, assets/ included - which is why those four washes are part of the lift rather than decoration. The chart's cursor was built by behaviour.js, the only javascript the system owns, and it works in the empty folder exactly as it works in the product.

And one dependency it cannot provide

base.css asks for Inter by name and nothing in the folder provides it: no @font-face, no file, no import. The lift test passed without noticing, because the machine it ran on has Inter installed, which is exactly the shape of failure a handoff exists to prevent. It is written down now in the two places a person looks - design/system/CLAUDE.md under "What this folder does NOT carry", and at the declaration in base.css, which is the line somebody greps for.

Five weights are actually used: a subset that drops the heaviest loses the wordmark. The fallback chain in base.css is deliberate and safe, so a page with no Inter is readable and not broken - it is simply not Tendd.
