The package boundary: what this is, and what it is not

This is a clickable product, not an application. There is no data layer, no API, no router, no persistence and no session. Nothing here talks to a bank, charges a card or remembers anything between two page loads. A reviewer can walk every flow end to end and a developer can read every rule, and neither of those is running software.

Every STATE is its own file. 17 screens carry 38 states between them and that is 57 html files: home.html, home-loading.html, home-empty.html, home-error.html, home-few.html, home-one.html are one screen. A real build collapses those 57 files back into 17 screens with states, and nothing in this repository says who owns that translation - it is the first decision a receiver makes and it is named here because it was written down nowhere.

Two scripts exist and they are not the same kind of thing. design/system/behaviour.js belongs to the system, is the only javascript it owns, travels with it, and draws the trend chart's cursor: 7 of the 57 screens load it. design/story.js belongs to the landing and drives its scroll choreography: 1 screen loads it. Everything else on every screen is html and css.

Which theme is the main one

Light is the main one, dark is complete, and since 2026-08-23 a person can choose. The light palette is the one written on :root; the dark palette is written on [data-theme="dark"] and overrides it role by role. Every semantic role in the system exists in both or it does not exist, which is a rule of stage 08 and is checked rather than promised.

The switch you see on a screen is not the one this is about. That one belongs to design/_screen.css, the reviewer's chrome, together with the panel that names the screen and its IA node. Chrome is not shipped, and the map of the product deliberately does not include it. The PRODUCT's control is a switch in a group of its own on Settings, How Tendd looks, above the rows that leave the screen.

This was the sharpest question a stranger asked, and for six hours the honest answer was that nobody had decided. The system had carried both themes since stage 08, and the product had no control, no string for one, and no prefers-color-scheme anywhere: a capability that was fully paid for and a feature that had never been specified. Three readings were available and the founder chose the third: follow the machine, stay light-only and say so, or give a person the choice. The two new lines went through Voice before they went on a screen, the block went into IA node 6.16, and the structure went into the frozen grey twin with the decision written into that file, which is the only way this project allows the structure contract to change after Voice.

And the product still does not follow the machine. prefers-color-scheme stays at zero occurrences in base.css, in tokens.css and in both chrome stylesheets, deliberately: a person who has chosen a theme has said what they want, and a money app that changes appearance because the sun went down is doing something its user did not ask for.

What a real build still owes is one line in a data model, and it is written in behaviour.md rather than guessed here: where the choice lives. On the account, so it follows a person to a second device, or in the browser, so it is per-device. The switch in this package draws the state and does not keep it, exactly like the four alert switches above it, because this is a clickable product with no runtime and no persistence.

The zoom failure was the width sweep's own lesson arriving one axis later, and the fix was the same stage answering itself. What closed it is the reason stage 10 refused to write its two width points in pixels: a container query in rem reads the viewport in units of the reader's text, so one threshold releases a label that may not fold, and it releases it under zoom and nowhere in the product's own sweep. The blind spot this stage could not close all day was that no instrument read a page which is not a screen, and it is closed. The accessibility run took the 57 coloured screens, the semantics run took those and their grey twins, and both stopped - while a receiver meets a stage page BEFORE any product screen, because the roadmap is how they arrive. It was two holes and not one, which is why it lasted: the corpus never included those pages, AND the contrast walker rooted itself at the product and returned nothing when it found none. Check 5 reads 129 pages and 41624 text nodes, and what it found was a shape rather than a tail: 2330 nodes below the floor, of which eight colour literals caused 2203, copied page to page across a documentation half written before the design system. Ten literals darkened took it to 6, and those six are text that DEMONSTRATES a colour on a page about colour. 87 of 129 pages had no main landmark and 0 do now.

And the instrument had to be made deterministic before any of its numbers could be believed. Its first two full runs reported catastrophic contrast failures, sixteen in one and twenty-eight in the next, on different screens each time, and none of them reproduced in isolation. The cause is in the system: every interactive element transitions its colours over the state duration, so asserting a theme starts a cross-fade across the whole page and a sample taken inside it compares one theme's ink against a surface halfway to the other. The contrast and focus passes now run with reduced motion emulated, which makes the product's own reduce block zero every duration; the motion pass needs a context of its own, since measuring the baseline inside a reduced one would report the reduce block as the product's motion, which is a check that always passes and proves nothing. Two consecutive runs now return byte-identical output, and that determinism is part of what "confirmed" means.

How to add a feature

There is a ready prompt for it: handoff/docs/one-shot.md. Copy it, put your feature in the one blank it leaves, and hand it to whoever is building - a person, or a model in a fresh session. It leads to all three documents above, carries the growth rules word for word from the files that own them, and repeats no value from any of them.

It was written to be tested, and it was tested twice on 2026-08-23 by two readers with no memory of this project, on two different features. Each was given the prompt and nothing else, and each was sized before the run rather than after it - four states and three - because a reader who does not stumble on a feature of two states has proved nothing. Both built something the instruments accepted: zero style violations, zero contrast or focus failures across the grown corpus in both themes, zero prose past the reading measure at fifty-eight widths.
